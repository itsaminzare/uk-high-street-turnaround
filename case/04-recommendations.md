# 4. Recommendations

## The answer, first

> **Northbridge should shrink its retail core and fill the rest of the town centre with reasons to visit and to live there. Shopping alone will not bring people back.**

This rests on three pillars, each built on the [findings](03-findings.md):

| Pillar | Why (finding) | What the council does |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Right-size the centre** | Too much retail space for the spending that is left (A, B) | Define a compact retail core of 2–3 streets. Rezone the edges for homes, health, education and workspace, and use the council's own property and capital to start conversions. |
| **2. Fill empty units fast** | Long-term empty units drag the centre down, and the council now has powers (D) | Run High Street Rental Auctions on units empty more than a year. Launch a meanwhile-use and pop-up programme. Offer a 2026/27 rates transition grant to keep independents open. |
| **3. Give people reasons to come** | Strong centres win on food, leisure and residents (A, D) | Create one food and leisure anchor, such as a market hall in a large empty unit. Run a year-round events programme. Prioritise new town-centre homes. |

## Prioritisation (80/20)

We scored each option on **impact** (contribution to the 18% → 14% target) and **ease** (cost, speed, and how far it's within council control). Scores are team judgement on a 1–5 scale, informed by the findings. A score of 3 or more counts as high.

| Option | Impact | Ease | Quadrant |
|---|:---:|:---:|---|
| High Street Rental Auctions on units empty more than a year | 4 | 4 | **Quick win** |
| 2026/27 rates transition grant for small independents | 3 | 3 | **Quick win** |
| Shrink the retail core: convert the edges to homes and services | 5 | 2 | **Big bet** |
| Food and leisure anchor (market hall) | 4 | 2 | **Big bet** |
| Meanwhile-use and pop-up programme | 2 | 5 | Fill-in |
| Town-centre data dashboard (vacancy, footfall, openings) | 2 | 4 | Fill-in (enabler for everything else) |
| Year-round events programme | 2 | 3 | Fill-in |
| Free parking | 1 | 2 | Money pit: costly, and doesn't address the shift online |

**Reading the matrix:** do both quick wins now, fund both big bets as the core of the five-year plan, run the fill-ins alongside because they are cheap, and drop free parking.

The interactive version of this matrix is in the [Issue Tree Studio](../tool/) tool.

## Roadmap

| When | Actions | Milestone |
|---|---|---|
| **First 12 months** | Launch the dashboard. Designate the High Street Rental Auction area and run the first 5–10 auctions. Start the pop-up programme. Open the rates transition grant. | Vacancy falls 1–2 points; persistent vacancy starts falling |
| **Years 1–3** | Adopt the town-centre masterplan (core plus conversion zones). Open the food and leisure anchor. Begin the first council-led conversions. | Footfall above the 2025 baseline; anchor open |
| **Years 3–5** | Deliver the conversions to homes and services. Shrink the retail footprint to match demand. | **Vacancy at 14% by 2030** |

## How we'll know it's working

| KPI | Baseline | 2030 target |
|---|---|---|
| Town-centre vacancy rate | ~18% | ≤ 14% |
| Persistent vacancy (empty 2+ years) | To be measured | Halved |
| Footfall | 2025 = 100 | > 100 |
| Share of units in food, leisure and services | To be measured | Up 10 points |
| New homes in the town centre | 0 | Set in masterplan |

## Risks and how to manage them

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Landlords resist auctions or conversions | Start with the longest-empty units, publish the data, and offer partnership before using compulsory powers |
| Capital budget is too small for conversions | Use council-owned property first; bid for national regeneration funding; use joint ventures with housing associations |
| Pop-ups don't turn into lasting tenants | Track conversion to longer leases; give successful pop-ups priority in the auctions |
| National policy changes again | Review the rates grant every year; keep the plan built on demand and supply, not on tax |

## Next steps

1. Present to cabinet, then agree the core boundary and the auction designation.
2. Commission a unit-by-unit vacancy survey to set exact baselines.
3. Run the first rental auction within six months, to show early results.
