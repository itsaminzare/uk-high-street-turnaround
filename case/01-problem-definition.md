# 1. Problem definition

## The situation

**Northbridge** is an illustrative northern English town, a composite of the high-vacancy town centres described in public data. It is not a real client. Its town-centre shop vacancy rate is about **18%**, in line with the North East average (17.9%, Q4 2023) and well above the GB average (14.0%). Footfall is flat to falling, chains are leaving, and from April 2026 the council's small independent retailers lose the 40% business rates relief they had in 2025/26.

The council leader has asked: *"What do we do about the high street?"* That is a worry, not a question we can answer, so the first job is to sharpen it.

## The key question

> **How can Northbridge Council bring town-centre shop vacancy down from about 18% to the GB average of 14% by 2030, while increasing footfall, using its existing powers and a capital budget of roughly £5m?**

| Test | How the question meets it |
|---|---|
| **Specific** | Town-centre ground-floor units in Northbridge, not the whole borough or online retail |
| **Measurable** | Vacancy rate (18% → 14%) and footfall against a 2025 baseline |
| **Action-oriented** | Asks what the council can *do*, not why the high street is declining |
| **Relevant** | Vacancy is the council's most visible problem and drives footfall, safety and rates income |
| **Time-bound** | By 2030, a five-year horizon matching a capital programme |

## Who decides, and what success looks like

| Stakeholder | What they need |
|---|---|
| Council leader and cabinet (decision-makers) | A plan they can fund, defend publicly and show progress on within a year |
| Town-centre landlords | Lettable space and stable rental income; protection of asset values |
| Independent retailers | Lower occupancy costs and more customers through the door |
| Residents | Reasons to come into town: services, food, events, homes |
| Business Improvement District (if any) | A clear role and data to show impact |

**Success criteria:** vacancy at or below 14% by 2030; persistent vacancy (empty for two or more years) halved; footfall above the 2025 baseline; at least one new anchor that brings people in for reasons other than shopping.

## Scope

**In scope:** the town-centre boundary; council powers (planning, High Street Rental Auctions, discretionary grants, its own property, events, parking); partnerships with landlords and the BID.

**Out of scope:** national tax policy (we take the 2026/27 business rates system as given), out-of-town retail parks, and online retail itself. We can't change the shift online, only respond to it.

## Constraints

- About £5m of capital over five years, with little ongoing revenue budget
- Most town-centre units are privately owned
- Results need to be visible within 12 months to keep political support
