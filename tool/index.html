Define the problem

Turn a vague worry into a question you can answer. A good key question is specific, measurable, action-oriented, relevant to a decision-maker and time-bound. The checklist updates as you type.

What is happening, and why does it matter now?
The one question the work must answer.
Who acts on the answer?
By when must it be achieved?
How will you know it worked? Use numbers.
Budget, time, powers, politics.

Is the question SMART?

    Structure it as an issue tree

    Break the question into branches that don't overlap and together cover everything (MECE). Give each branch a hypothesis to test, then record the evidence and whether it held up.

    Key question

      Prioritise the options

      Score each option for impact on the goal and ease (cost, speed, control). Scores of 3 or more count as high. Do the quick wins now, plan the big bets, keep the cheap fill-ins, and drop the money pits.

      #OptionImpactEaseQuadrant

      Impact / ease matrix

      Numbers match the table. Points with the same scores are spread slightly so they stay readable.

      Synthesise the storyline

      Lead with the answer. Write the governing thought (your recommendation in one sentence), then 2–4 key lines that each support it, each backed by evidence. This is the pyramid principle: the reader gets the answer first and the reasons second.
